Case 2

Paper Diagnostics for Viral Disease

Pardee / Collins Labs · Clinical trial: Recife, Brazil

A portable test for viral outbreaks — originally developed for Zika — giving lab-grade accuracy without a PCR machine or a cold supply chain.

98.5%
accuracy vs. CDC gold-standard PCR
$5
per test, used live in the 2015–16 outbreak in Recife, Brazil
268
patient samples tested during active outbreak

The technology

  • Cell-free RNA sensors, freeze-dried onto paper, with a colorimetric readout
  • PLUM reader: ~$500, portable
  • Programmable to pathogen sequences

What made it field-deployable

  • Fiocruz as partner from planning stage, so in-country expertise shaped the design
  • Tested during the 2015–2016 outbreak
  • No PCR supply chain dependency
  • Visual readout, minimal training

What the partnership looked like

  • Fiocruz, the Brazilian public health institute, was a partner from the planning stage — not brought in after the fact
  • In-country expertise shaped what the design needed to be
  • It later became a startup (En Carta Diagnostics, Paris) — a real path from field pilot to lasting local capacity

Why this case matters

  • Tested during active outbreak
  • Demonstrates real-world urgency tests
  • Complete arc: lab → field → commercial

"Access to bioreagents is a real limit in the Global South — antibodies and enzymes get stuck in customs, and cost far more once they arrive."

Keith Pardee · University of Toronto — Paper Diagnostics
